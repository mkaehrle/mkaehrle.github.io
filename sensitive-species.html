Draft snapshot · August 2026

This work-in-progress dataset compiles publicly available information from several dozen biodiversity data providers that have implemented automatic data access restrictions for sensitive species occurrence data. Its focus is solely automatic restrictions for designated sensitive species; discretionary restrictions fall outside its scope.

The core unit of the dataset is a restriction: a treatment such as generalization applied to data about a particular taxon under certain geographic or temporal conditions. A restriction may be documented by one or more restriction observations, instances in which a source confirms that the restriction remains in place. If a species is delisted, the restriction lapses. If restrictions are later reinstated, they are recorded as new restrictions. A change to the treatment or geographic scope also creates a new restriction.

The dataset supports comparisons of how data providers treat sensitive species information across taxonomy, geography, and time. It can be used to examine which taxa are commonly restricted, which treatments or rationales are most prevalent, how practices differ across providers, and how individual providers’ practices develop over time.

We harmonized data from sources including published static lists, biodiversity portal APIs, and dynamic lists such as species filters in participatory science platforms. Collection methods ranged from direct data downloads to custom web-crawling applications.

We will publish the dataset tables in addition to all supporting code, allowing users to obtain the raw data if desired through our ingestion pipeline. We have archived sources using the Wayback Machine where possible and drawn on existing Wayback captures for historical comparisons.

A public portal may eventually provide one way to explore a released version of the dataset.
